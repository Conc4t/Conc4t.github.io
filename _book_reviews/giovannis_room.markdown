Giovanni's Room was a melancholic mood piece for me. Praised for its passionate and daring depiction of gay romance, it is also a tale of fated tragedy set in the cold "mist and rain of Paris" told through David's "still" reflection. The beginnings of both parts make no attempt to hide this. 

It is not a story of us against the world but one of falling, of prisons, of love in a messy and claustrophic room, of a confused David, alone, while Hella is on the high seas and Giovanni about to perish.

Baldwin's poetic and elegant prose (I like in particular his clever Biblical references), combined with a narrator "too various to be trusted", makes Giovanni's Room an enjoyable read but also a difficult one for me to extract anything black and white from. However, what can I expect from an author who himself defied labels?

"... a real decision makes one humble, one knows that it is at the mercy of more things than can be named..."

"Everyone, after all, goes the same dark road — and the road has a trick of being most dark, most treacherous, when it seems most bright — and it's true that nobody stays in the Garden of Eden."

I've been noticing and thinking about the same fundamanetal thing in literature lately — authorial control over what the reader experiences:

Baldwin can make a room feel hot and cold and psychologically suffocating at the same time.

Dostoevsky can make a thought spiral until you feel trapped inside someone's consciousness.

Garcia Marquez can make the implausible feel mundane. 

And Stoker has a way of creating suspense and miasma like no other author I've encountered. 

"Lucy has not walked much in her sleep the last week, but there is an odd concentration about her which I do not understand; even in her sleep she seems to be watching me. She tries the door, and finding it locked, goes about the room searching for the key."

Why is this subtle little imagery so unsettling?
